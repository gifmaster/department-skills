# Responsible use

These skills make AI assistants more useful for business work. They do not remove the need for human judgement, professional advice or compliance with the law. Read this before rolling them out to a team.

## Principles built into every skill

1. **People decide.** Skills prepare analysis, drafts and options. Decisions about people, money, contracts and customers stay with a named person.
2. **No invented facts.** Every skill tells the AI not to invent figures, sources, legal references, customers or quotes, and to list assumptions and open questions.
3. **Data minimisation.** Skills ask users to remove personal data that is not needed and never to share passwords or secrets.
4. **Verify the law.** EU and Danish notes are orientation. Law, thresholds and dates change, and collective agreements or contracts can change the answer.

## Skills that need extra care

| Area | Skills | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment, promotion and performance | `hr-candidate-screening-rubric`, `hr-performance-review-writer`, `cs-qa-scorecard` | Under the AI Act (Annex III, point 4), AI used to select candidates or evaluate workers is high-risk. High-risk requirements apply from 2 December 2027 (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744), but prohibitions and AI literacy duties already apply. Use these skills to support a human reviewer, not to decide. Inform candidates and employees, and check whether your use makes you a deployer of a high-risk system. |
| Legal | All `legal-` skills | Legal information, not legal advice. Use qualified review for disputes, regulator contact and high-value contracts. |
| Tax and accounting | `fin-vat-return-prep`, `fin-accounting-policy-memo`, `fin-annual-report-prep` | Working papers for review by a responsible accountant, auditor or tax adviser. |
| Security incidents and breaches | `it-incident-triage`, `it-phishing-triage`, `legal-data-breach-response` | Time-critical legal clocks (GDPR 72 hours, NIS2 24 hours). Follow your incident plan and involve the right people early. |
| Customer-facing AI | `cs-chatbot-faq-design`, `sales-prospecting-sequence` | Tell people when they interact with AI (AI Act Article 50) and respect marketing consent rules. |

## Before rolling out to a team

- Agree which AI tools are approved and which data may be entered into them (see `hr-policy-drafter` and `it-ai-use-case-intake`).
- Train staff in AI literacy, as required by AI Act Article 4 for organisations that deploy AI.
- Adapt the catalogue to your own policies, approval limits and templates, then rebuild.
- Record which skills are used for which processes in your AI use case register.
- Review outputs regularly and collect feedback to improve the skills.
