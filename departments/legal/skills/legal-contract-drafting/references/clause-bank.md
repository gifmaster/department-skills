# Clause bank: B2B service and consultancy agreement (Danish law)
Use: read when drafting or reviewing a service, consultancy or professional services agreement under Danish law, to take balanced model clauses and pick the customer-friendly or supplier-friendly variant for liability, IP and termination.

Legal information and drafting support, not legal advice. Clauses are balanced starting points in English. Replace [placeholders], keep defined terms consistent, and have a lawyer review high-value or regulated deals. Variants marked [CUSTOMER] favour the buyer; [SUPPLIER] favour the provider.

## Drafting checklist for consultancy deals

- [ ] Correct legal entities and registration numbers (CVR or foreign equivalent); check the legal form (for example a company versus a sole trader).
- [ ] Disguised employment risk: one named person, fixed monthly fee, full-time, client's equipment and working hours. Keep scope deliverable-based and the consultant in control of method.
- [ ] IP: consultant's personnel must assign their rights to the consultant so that the assignment to the customer works.
- [ ] Personal data: Art. 28 DPA in place before any access; sub-processor approval from the customer's own clients if needed.
- [ ] Payment terms max 30 days unless expressly agreed (renteloven § 3a).
- [ ] Liability cap and IP clause reflect which side the user is on.

## 1. Parties and definitions

> This Agreement is made between [Customer name], CVR [number], [address] (the "Customer") and [Supplier name], CVR [number], [address] (the "Supplier"), each a "Party".
>
> In this Agreement:
> "Agreement" means these terms and the Schedules. If there is a conflict, these terms prevail over the Schedules, except Schedule [3] (Data Processing Agreement), which prevails on data protection.
> "Services" means the services described in Schedule 1.
> "Deliverables" means all documents, code, reports, models and other materials that the Supplier delivers or is required to deliver under this Agreement.
> "Background IP" means intellectual property rights owned by or licensed to a Party before the Effective Date or developed independently of this Agreement.
> "Confidential Information" has the meaning given in clause 9.
> "Personal Data", "Controller" and "Processor" have the meanings in Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR).
> "Effective Date" means [date].
> "Business Day" means a day on which banks in Denmark are open for general business.

## 2. Scope and performance

> 2.1 The Supplier shall provide the Services and Deliverables as described in Schedule 1, in accordance with good industry practice and with the skill and care expected of a professional supplier of similar services.
> 2.2 The Supplier decides how the Services are carried out, subject to the agreed deliverables, deadlines and reasonable instructions on security and access.
> 2.3 The Customer shall provide the information, access, decisions and cooperation set out in Schedule 1 in good time. The Supplier is not responsible for delay caused by the Customer's failure to do so, and deadlines are extended accordingly.
> 2.4 [Key Personnel: The Services shall be performed by [name]. The Supplier may replace Key Personnel only with the Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.]

## 3. Acceptance

> 3.1 The Customer shall test each Deliverable designated for acceptance in Schedule 1 within [10] Business Days of delivery (the "Acceptance Period") against the acceptance criteria in Schedule 1.
> 3.2 If a Deliverable does not meet the acceptance criteria, the Customer shall notify the Supplier in writing within the Acceptance Period, describing the deviations. The Supplier shall correct them and redeliver within a reasonable time, after which a new Acceptance Period applies.
> 3.3 A Deliverable is deemed accepted when the Customer confirms acceptance in writing, when the Acceptance Period expires without a notice under clause 3.2, or when the Customer uses the Deliverable in live operation, whichever is earliest.
> 3.4 If a Deliverable fails acceptance [twice] for reasons attributable to the Supplier, the Customer may terminate the affected part of the Agreement and recover fees paid for it.

## 4. Fees and payment

> 4.1 The Customer shall pay the fees set out in Schedule 2. All fees are exclusive of VAT.
> 4.2 The Supplier invoices [monthly in arrears / on the milestones in Schedule 2]. Payment is due [30] days from the invoice date.
> 4.3 If the Customer fails to pay on time, the Supplier may charge interest under the Danish Interest Act (renteloven) and the fixed compensation for recovery costs provided by that Act.
> 4.4 The Customer may dispute an invoice in good faith by written notice before the due date, stating its reasons, and shall pay the undisputed part on time.
> 4.5 Expenses are reimbursed only if approved in writing in advance and documented.
> 4.6 [Indexation: the Supplier may adjust hourly rates once a year from [1 January] by no more than the change in Statistics Denmark's net price index over the previous 12 months, on [60] days' notice.]

Drafting notes:
- Payment terms over 30 days are only valid if expressly agreed and not grossly unfair to the creditor (renteloven § 3a; facts file). For public authority customers, 30 days (renteloven § 3b).
- Statutory late interest is the Nationalbanken reference rate plus 8 percentage points (renteloven § 5); fixed compensation currently DKK 310 per claim (renteloven § 9 a, stk. 3, verify current amount).
- Foreign supplier: add reverse charge VAT wording and confirm with the finance team.

## 5. Change control

> 5.1 Either Party may request a change to the Services in writing. Within [5] Business Days the Supplier shall provide a change proposal stating the impact on scope, deliverables, timetable, fees and risks.
> 5.2 No change is binding until both Parties have signed a change order. Until then the Supplier continues to perform the Services as agreed.
> 5.3 Changes required by a change in law are handled under this clause; each Party bears its own costs of assessing the change.
> 5.4 The Supplier may not refuse a reasonable change request but is entitled to an adjustment of fees and timetable in line with Schedule 2 rates.

## 6. Warranties

> 6.1 The Supplier warrants that (a) it will perform the Services in accordance with clause 2.1; (b) the Deliverables will materially conform to Schedule 1 for [90] days after acceptance; (c) it has the right to grant the rights in clause 8; and (d) it will comply with applicable law in performing the Services.
> 6.2 If the Supplier breaches clause 6.1(b), it shall remedy the defect at its own cost within a reasonable time. If it fails to do so, the Customer may obtain a proportionate price reduction or, for material defects, terminate under clause 13.3.
> 6.3 Except as stated in this Agreement, no warranties are given, express or implied.

Drafting note: Danish law has no general implied warranty regime for services equivalent to sale of goods; the parties' agreement and general principles of contract law apply. State remedies expressly.

## 7. Liability cap and exclusions

Balanced:
> 7.1 Each Party is liable for losses caused by its breach in accordance with the general rules of Danish law, subject to this clause 7.
> 7.2 Neither Party is liable for indirect or consequential loss, including loss of profit, loss of revenue, loss of business, loss of goodwill, loss of data (other than the cost of restoring data from the latest backup) and losses arising from claims of third parties.
> 7.3 Each Party's total liability under or in connection with this Agreement is limited to an amount equal to the fees paid and payable in the [12] months before the event giving rise to the claim [, and in any event not less than DKK [amount]].
> 7.4 The limitations in clauses 7.2 and 7.3 do not apply to (a) liability for gross negligence or wilful misconduct; (b) breach of clause 9 (Confidentiality); (c) a Party's obligations under clause 8.5 (IP indemnity); or (d) the Customer's obligation to pay fees.
> 7.5 [Data protection: liability for breach of clause 10 and the Data Processing Agreement is limited to [2 times the cap in clause 7.3].]

[CUSTOMER] variant:
> 7.3 The Supplier's total liability is limited to [the higher of 150% of the total fees under this Agreement and DKK [amount]]. The limitations do not apply to breach of clause 10 (Data Protection), and losses from a personal data breach caused by the Supplier, including regulatory fines to the extent recoverable by law, costs of notification and reasonable remediation costs, are direct losses.

[SUPPLIER] variant:
> 7.3 The Supplier's total liability is limited to the fees paid in the [6] months before the event giving rise to the claim. Clause 7.4(b) and (c) apply only up to [3 times] that amount. Claims must be notified in writing within [12] months of the Customer becoming aware of the circumstances giving rise to them.

Drafting notes:
- "Indirect loss" has no fixed statutory meaning in Danish law. List the excluded heads of loss expressly, as in 7.2.
- Limitations are generally not upheld for gross negligence or intent (verify); 7.4(a) reflects this.
- Check the cap against the Supplier's professional indemnity insurance and require evidence of cover.

## 8. Intellectual property

Balanced:
> 8.1 Each Party retains its Background IP.
> 8.2 On payment of the relevant fees, the Customer obtains [ownership of / an exclusive licence to] the Deliverables created specifically for the Customer, including the right to modify, develop and assign them.
> 8.3 The Supplier grants the Customer a non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free licence to use any Supplier Background IP embedded in the Deliverables, to the extent necessary to use the Deliverables for the Customer's business.
> 8.4 The Supplier may reuse general know-how, methods and tools that do not contain the Customer's Confidential Information.
> 8.5 The Supplier shall indemnify the Customer against third party claims that the Deliverables infringe intellectual property rights, provided the Customer notifies the claim promptly, lets the Supplier control the defence and cooperates reasonably. The Supplier may modify or replace the Deliverable to avoid infringement.
> 8.6 The Supplier shall procure that its personnel and subcontractors transfer to the Supplier all rights necessary for clauses 8.2 and 8.3 to take effect.
> 8.7 The Supplier shall not include open source components under licences that would require disclosure of the Customer's code without the Customer's prior written consent.

[CUSTOMER] variant:
> 8.2 All rights, including copyright and the right to modify, develop and assign onwards, in the Deliverables and all work products created under this Agreement pass to the Customer on creation, without further payment.

Drafting note: under Danish copyright law the transfer covers only the uses the parties expressly contemplated unless otherwise agreed (specificeringsgrundsætningen; ophavsretsloven § 53, stk. 3 (verify)). State the right to modify and assign onwards expressly; without agreement the transferee may not transfer the copyright onwards (ophavsretsloven § 56 (verify)).

[SUPPLIER] variant:
> 8.2 The Supplier retains all rights in the Deliverables. On full payment, the Customer obtains a non-exclusive, non-transferable, perpetual licence to use the Deliverables for its internal business purposes. Modifications by or for the Customer are at its own risk.

## 9. Confidentiality

> 9.1 "Confidential Information" means all information disclosed by a Party in connection with this Agreement that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential.
> 9.2 The receiving Party shall keep Confidential Information confidential, use it only to perform this Agreement and disclose it only to employees, advisers and subcontractors who need to know it and are bound by equivalent duties.
> 9.3 Clause 9.2 does not apply to information that is or becomes public without breach, was lawfully known before disclosure, is independently developed or is lawfully received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality, or to disclosure required by law or a competent authority.
> 9.4 This clause applies during the term and for [3] years after its end; for trade secrets for as long as they remain trade secrets.

## 10. Data protection

> 10.1 To the extent the Supplier processes Personal Data on behalf of the Customer, the Supplier acts as Processor and the Parties shall comply with the Data Processing Agreement in Schedule [3], which meets Article 28(3) GDPR. No Personal Data may be processed before it is signed.
> 10.2 The Supplier shall not transfer Personal Data to a country outside the EU/EEA without the Customer's prior written approval and a valid transfer mechanism under Chapter V GDPR.
> 10.3 Each Party is a separate Controller for Personal Data it processes for its own purposes, such as contact details of the other Party's staff.

Drafting notes: use the Commission's standard contractual clauses for Art. 28 (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915) or Datatilsynet's adopted standard clauses as Schedule 3 (facts file). Check whether the Customer's own clients must approve the Supplier as a sub-processor.

## 11. Subcontractors

> 11.1 The Supplier may use subcontractors only with the Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. Subcontractors listed in Schedule [4] are approved.
> 11.2 The Supplier remains responsible for its subcontractors as for itself.
> 11.3 Sub-processors of Personal Data are governed by the Data Processing Agreement.

## 12. Term

> 12.1 This Agreement takes effect on the Effective Date and continues [until [date] / until terminated under clause 13].

## 13. Termination

Balanced:
> 13.1 Either Party may terminate this Agreement for convenience on [3 months'] written notice to the end of a calendar month [, but not to expire before [date]].
> 13.2 Either Party may terminate with immediate effect by written notice if the other Party commits a material breach and, where remediable, fails to remedy it within [15] Business Days of a written notice describing the breach and referring to this clause.
> 13.3 Either Party may terminate with immediate effect if the other Party enters bankruptcy, reconstruction or similar insolvency proceedings, to the extent permitted by law.
> 13.4 On termination the Customer pays for Services performed and approved expenses incurred up to the termination date.

[CUSTOMER] variant:
> 13.1 The Customer may terminate for convenience on [30] days' notice. The Supplier may terminate for convenience only on [6] months' notice. On termination for convenience by the Customer, the Customer pays for Services performed up to the termination date and documented, unavoidable committed costs, but no compensation for lost profit.

[SUPPLIER] variant:
> 13.1 Neither Party may terminate for convenience before [date]. On early termination by the Customer other than under clause 13.2 or 13.3, the Customer pays [the fees for the remainder of the minimum term / a termination fee of [x]% of remaining fees].
> 13.5 The Supplier may suspend the Services if any undisputed amount is more than [30] days overdue and remains unpaid [10] Business Days after a written reminder.

Drafting note: Danish law also allows termination for material breach (væsentlig misligholdelse) without an express clause, but a defined cure period gives certainty.

## 14. Exit assistance

> 14.1 On expiry or termination, the Supplier shall for up to [3] months, at the Customer's request and at the rates in Schedule 2, provide reasonable assistance to transfer the Services to the Customer or a new supplier, including handing over Deliverables, documentation and data in a commonly used, machine-readable format.
> 14.2 The Supplier shall return or delete the Customer's data as instructed and confirm deletion in writing, save for copies required by law.
> 14.3 Exit assistance is provided even if the Agreement is terminated for the Customer's breach, provided the Customer pays in advance.

## 15. Force majeure

> 15.1 Neither Party is liable for failure to perform caused by circumstances beyond its reasonable control which it could not reasonably have foreseen or avoided, such as war, natural disaster, epidemic, general labour dispute, government action or failure of public infrastructure ("Force Majeure"). Labour disputes affecting only the Party's own staff, and failures of a subcontractor, are Force Majeure only if the subcontractor itself is affected by Force Majeure.
> 15.2 The affected Party shall notify the other without undue delay and take reasonable steps to limit the effect.
> 15.3 If Force Majeure lasts more than [30] days, either Party may terminate the Agreement by written notice without liability.

## 16. Assignment

> Neither Party may assign or transfer its rights or obligations without the other Party's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. Either Party may, however, assign to an affiliate or to an acquirer of the relevant business that assumes the obligations in writing, on written notice. The Supplier may assign receivables to a financing institution.

## 17. Entire agreement and amendments

> 17.1 This Agreement constitutes the entire agreement between the Parties on its subject matter and supersedes all prior agreements, proposals and representations. Nothing in this clause limits liability for fraud.
> 17.2 Amendments are valid only if in writing and signed by both Parties. [Email exchanges do not amend this Agreement unless they expressly state that they do and are confirmed by authorised signatories.]
> 17.3 Neither Party's standard terms apply, even if referred to in an order or invoice.

## 18. Governing law and venue

Court option:
> This Agreement is governed by Danish law, excluding its choice of law rules. Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement shall be settled by the Copenhagen City Court (Københavns Byret) as the court of first instance.

Arbitration option:
> Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, including disputes regarding its existence or validity, shall be finally settled by arbitration administered by the Danish Institute of Arbitration (Voldgiftsinstituttet) in accordance with its rules in force when the proceedings are commenced. The arbitral tribunal shall consist of [one / three] arbitrator(s) appointed by the Institute. The seat of arbitration is Copenhagen and the language is [English].

Drafting notes:
- Courts are cheaper for smaller claims; arbitration gives confidentiality, a chosen language and easier enforcement abroad (New York Convention). Check current institute rules and model clause wording on the institute's website (verify).
- Agreements on venue are generally permitted in business contracts (retsplejeloven § 245 (verify)).
